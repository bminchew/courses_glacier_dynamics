Glaciers tend to exist with effective deviatoric stresses of ~100 kPa, which happens to be near the crossover stress between GBS-accommodated basal slip and dislocation creep. This crossover is set by the material properties of ice. The coincidence is physically meaningful: the interplay of GBS and dislocation creep, and the viscosity they produce, partially controls the stress state of glaciers, causing stresses to cluster near the boundary between the two mechanisms.

**Why:** This is a key conceptual insight for the ice rheology lecture — it connects the microphysics of creep mechanisms to the macroscopic stress state of glaciers.
**How to apply:** When discussing Figure 4 or the composite flow law, emphasize that the ~100 kPa glacier stress scale is not independent of the rheology — there is a feedback between the creep mechanism, the effective viscosity, and the resulting stress state.
